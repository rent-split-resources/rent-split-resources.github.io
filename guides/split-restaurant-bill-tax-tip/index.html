How to split a restaurant bill with tax and tip

A restaurant bill has more than a food subtotal. Agree on shared dishes, allocate the actual tax, and check whether a service charge or gratuity is already included.

THE STARTING POINT

For an itemized split, assign each person's items first, then allocate tax and an agreed tip using a consistent rule. Reconcile against the final receipt.

1. Decide between equal and itemized

An equal split is straightforward when orders are similar and everyone agrees. If the final total including tax and the agreed tip is $144, four people pay $36 each.

An itemized split can work better when one person orders much less or does not share alcohol. Assign each person's items, divide shared dishes among the people who shared them, and allocate discounts before calculating each subtotal. Those subtotals should add up to the discounted food-and-drink total.

2. Allocate tax and tip consistently

When all items have the same tax treatment, one approach is to allocate the receipt's actual tax in proportion to each person's pre-tax subtotal. If a person's items make up 25% of that subtotal, they pay 25% of the tax. Different tax rates or exemptions may require item-level tax instead.

Agree on the tip amount or percentage and its base. A tip calculated on the pre-tax amount differs from a tip calculated on a tax-inclusive total. Local customs and restaurant policies vary; the example below uses 20% of the pre-tax subtotal as a household choice.

Person's total = item subtotal + allocated tax + allocated tip + allocated extra charges

3. Follow an itemized example

Three diners have pre-tax item subtotals of $30, $45, and $25, for a $100 subtotal. The actual tax is $8. The group agrees to a $20 tip, equal to 20% of the pre-tax subtotal, and there is no existing service charge.

Proportional allocation of a $128 restaurant bill
DinerItemsTaxTipTotal
A$30.00$2.40$6.00$38.40
B$45.00$3.60$9.00$57.60
C$25.00$2.00$5.00$32.00
Total$100.00$8.00$20.00$128.00

If diners instead agreed to an equal split, the final $128 would divide into $42.67, $42.67, and $42.66. Reconcile the last cent so the server receives the intended total.

4. Read existing charges before adding a tip

Look for a service charge, automatic gratuity, delivery fee, or other added charge. Do not assume that every fee is a tip or that a service charge goes directly to the staff. Ask the restaurant when the receipt is unclear.

If a gratuity is already included and the group decides it covers the intended tip, allocate that charge once. Do not add the same intended tip again automatically. If the group chooses an additional gratuity, record it as an additional amount.

5. Settle with the person who paid

If one diner pays the card total, the others reimburse that diner for their calculated shares. Deduct any cash or deposits already contributed before asking for another payment. Keep the receipt until everyone has settled.

Common questions

Should tip be calculated before or after tax?

Agree on a base and follow any applicable local practice or restaurant policy. This guide's example uses the pre-tax subtotal; it is not a universal rule.

How should shared appetizers be split?

Divide them among the diners who agreed to share them, then add those portions to each person's item subtotal before allocating tax and tip.
